# Scenario audit — every situation in the app

Reviewed line by line. Bases, outs, who fields it, the answer the app marks
best, what else earns partial credit, and the reason shown to the player.

## The rules every answer follows

1. **No force beyond first** (nobody on, or first base empty) — throw to first.
   A runner who isn't forced can just go back, so there's nothing to chase.
2. **Force at second only** (runner on first, or first and third) — go to second.
   It's the lead runner AND it starts the double play. Both reasons point the same way.
3. **Force at third available** (first and second) — this is the one that trips people up.
   The third baseman or catcher takes third, because the bag is right there and it costs
   no throw. Everybody else goes to second, because second-then-first gets TWO outs and
   the force at third only gets one.
4. **Bases loaded, under two outs** — home. Home-to-first is still a double play, and it's
   the only play that also keeps the run off the board. With one out, second-then-first
   also ends the inning cleanly (a force out at first for the third out scores nobody),
   so that earns partial credit.
5. **Two outs** — take the surest out, which is usually first. Exception: if a force bag
   is literally under your foot (third baseman with a force at third, catcher with the
   bases loaded), step on it instead of throwing.

| Runner on second takes off for third. Who covers third base? | **Third baseman** | The third baseman stays on his own bag for a steal. He's the closest man and he already knows where the bag is without looking. |
| Runner steals second and the catcher's throw skips past the bag. Where should the pitcher be? | **Backing up third base** | If the throw gets away, that runner is going to third. The pitcher's job on every steal is to get behind third and keep him from scoring. |
| Runner on second breaks for third. Which outfielder backs up the throw to third? | **Left fielder** | Left field is directly behind third, so he charges in. The pitcher gets over there too, but the outfielder is the one deep enough to stop an overthrow from turning into a run. |
| Ground ball to the second baseman with nobody on base. Who covers first? | **First baseman** | He's already standing there. The pitcher only comes over when the ball pulls the first baseman off the bag. |
| Runner on first. Base hit into left field and he's rounding second for third. Who is the cutoff man for the throw to third? | **Shortstop** | The shortstop goes out toward left to meet the throw. The third baseman has to stay home on his bag or there's nobody there to catch it. |
| Fly ball to left field, runner tagging from third. Who is the cutoff man for the throw home? | **Third baseman** | From left field it's the third baseman — he's already on that side of the diamond and the throw comes right down his line. From center or right it's the first baseman instead. |
| Fly ball to left field, runner tagging from third, and the third baseman goes out to cut the throw. Who covers third base? | **Shortstop** | Somebody always has to be on the bag. When the third baseman leaves to be the cutoff, the shortstop slides over and takes his place. |

## Known judgment calls

These are defensible either way. If your league or your coaching differs, they're
one-line changes in `src/App.jsx`.

- **Runner on first, under two outs, ball to the catcher on a dribbler.** Marked as a
  throw to second. Some coaches want the sure out at first from a catcher, since it's a
  long throw. Second is still the lead force and starts the double play.
- **Runners on first and second, ball to the pitcher or shortstop.** Marked as second
  (double play) with third earning partial credit. A coach who teaches 'always get the
  lead runner' would flip these.
- **Bases loaded, nobody out.** Marked as home. Some coaches turn two instead and
  concede the run; home-to-first gets the same two outs without the run, so home wins here.
- **Two outs with a force at second, ball to the shortstop or second baseman.** Marked as
  first, with second earning partial credit. If he's standing on the bag, second is fine.
